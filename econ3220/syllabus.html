Course description

This course is the step after principles of macroeconomics. We study long-run growth and short-run fluctuations with models that start from household and firm decisions. You will use algebra and basic calculus (constrained optimization) to solve simple versions of those models and to discuss policy.

Prerequisite: ECON 2220 (and the usual math preparation for intermediate theory).

Learning goals

  • Measure GDP, prices, and labor-market outcomes carefully.
  • Solve a simple general-equilibrium macro model and explain what the equilibrium is doing.
  • Compare growth models (Malthus, Solow, endogenous growth) and growth accounting.
  • Analyze consumption, saving, credit-market frictions, and fiscal policy (including Ricardian equivalence).
  • Connect business-cycle models to monetary and fiscal policy debates.

Textbook

Stephen D. Williamson, Macroeconomics, 5th edition. Earlier editions are acceptable; chapter numbers follow the 5th edition. A legal copy is required. Publisher slides and the book PDF are not posted on this site.
