Course description

Theories and applications of economic development and growth, with emphasis on problems facing developing countries. Domestic topics include per-capita GDP and its growth, income distribution, population, unemployment, urbanization, education, and fiscal and financial policy. International topics include trade, foreign investment, and foreign debt. Financial and debt crises and recovery are discussed. Graduate students (8666) receive a different exam.

Prerequisites: ECON 2200 and ECON 2220 (and MATH 1310 or 1220 with a C or better, or permission of the instructor).

Learning goals

  • Describe development patterns and the usual ways we measure living standards.
  • Use exogenous and endogenous growth theory to organize evidence on why countries grow.
  • Analyze poverty, inequality, population, education, and health as development issues.
  • Evaluate saving, investment, fiscal, financial, and external-debt policies.

Textbook

Required: Dwight H. Perkins, Steven Radelet, David L. Lindauer, and Steven A. Block, Economics of Development, 7th edition, W. W. Norton, 2012.

Useful references (buy or library — not posted here): Anne Case and Angus Deaton, Deaths of Despair and the Future of Capitalism; Abhijit V. Banerjee and Esther Duflo, Poor Economics.
