Essay question (all students)

Since 1960 some poor countries have grown rapidly and others have not. Using the material we have covered, write a single coherent essay that answers:

What determines whether a poor country can raise living standards over a generation, and why is faster GDP growth not enough by itself?

A complete answer will do all of the following. You may use these as an outline, but write in essay form, not as a numbered worksheet.

  1. Measurement. Distinguish economic growth from economic development. Explain why GDP (or GNI) per capita, even in PPP terms, is an incomplete measure of living standards. Bring in at least one other indicator we used (for example literacy, life expectancy, or a composite index such as HDI).
  2. Sources of growth. Use the Solow framework (and, if useful, one endogenous-growth idea) to organize the roles of physical capital, labor, and productivity (TFP). Relate this to growth accounting: what the decomposition can and cannot tell us. You may refer to the China / India / Korea / Thailand exercise from Problem Set 1 if it helps.
  3. Poverty and inequality. Explain the difference between poverty and inequality. Discuss one channel through which a very unequal distribution can slow development, and one reason growth might still reduce poverty even if inequality does not fall.
  4. People. Use the demographic transition and human capital (education and/or health) to explain how population change and investment in people can raise or stall living standards. Be concrete about a mechanism (for example dependency ratios, returns to schooling, or the quality of schooling versus years of schooling).

Close with a short judgment: if you had to name the binding constraint for a typical low-income country in our readings, would you emphasize physical capital, productivity, demography, or human capital—and why? One paragraph is enough.

Additional part (ECON 8666 only)

In about one extra page, take a stand on this claim:

“If TFP growth is the main remaining difference between rich and poor countries, then the highest-return policy is whatever raises TFP, not more machines or more classrooms.”

Use the Solow logic (diminishing returns, catch-up, the residual) and at least one human-capital or poverty argument from class. You may agree, disagree, or qualify the claim, but you must say what evidence would change your mind.
